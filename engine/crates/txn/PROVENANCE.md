## Differences from the TypeScript engine

- **One call.** The TypeScript manager plans and applies in two calls bound by a plan hash. The engine plans and applies in one call under one hold of the workspace lock and writes the same journal: a `planned` revision with its first protected head, then `applying`, the replacement batches, and `applied` (reclaimed at once, as the autonomous path does) or `rolled_back`/`failed` (kept until they expire, as the manager keeps them). Dry-run and post-write validation stay with the caller: a plan with blocking diagnostics is refused, and the written bytes are verified against their hashes.
- **Same journal, key, heads and lock.** Journals are `<cache>/transactions/<id>/manifest.json` with `blobs/`, authenticated with the server's `journal-hmac.key` (passed in the request as `journalKey`) and recorded as protected heads under the server state root. Either side recovers the other's interrupted journal (`tests/unit/txn-engine.test.ts`). The lock is `<cache>/locks/write.lock` with the same `owner.json`.
- **Journal records.** The engine's plan carries no operations, read dependencies or artifacts, so those lists are empty; its validation summary records one check, `engine.source-hashes`. The diffs of the TypeScript plan belong to the artifacts packet (P3).
- **Pending intents.** The manifest schema caps `pendingFiles` at 16 entries while batches grow to 256 for large rewrites, so the TypeScript loader refuses the journal of a large rewrite interrupted mid-batch. The engine accepts up to 256, its largest batch.
- **A test crash.** `HOI4_TXN_TEST_ABORT_AFTER_REPLACE=<n>` makes the engine abort right after replacing file `n`, so the cross-recovery tests get a real crashed process. Nothing else reads it.
