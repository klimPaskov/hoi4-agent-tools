# ZIN map pipeline plan (2026-10-19)

Question: what must the map tool do to turn a written geography model of Erdae into an excellent Hearts of Iron IV map, and why did the current ZIN map come out weak and artificial?
Method: the map code (`src/hoi4_agent_tools/map/`, about 21,000 lines) read end to end for the paths `create_world` uses; the generated ZIN map (`map/*.bmp`, `definition.csv`, `history/states`, localisation) measured offline; the installed game (1.19.3.0 c01a: `map/`, `common/terrain/`, `common/defines/`, `gfx/FX/*.shader`, `hoi4.exe` strings) measured and read for engine facts; the offline wiki snapshot and the `hoi4-map-states` skill for documented rules. The game was never launched. Nothing in the repository was changed apart from this plan and [tool-gaps.md](tool-gaps.md).
Evidence marks used below: **V** verified here from installed game files, shader source, engine strings or measured data; **W** stated by the offline wiki or the skill, not re-verified; **U** unverified inference, with the test that would settle it.
All pixel measurements are on the ZIN map as written on 2026-10-18 (5,120 by 2,560 pixels, 7,108 provinces, seed 73) and on the installed vanilla map (5,632 by 2,048, 13,413 provinces). The analysis scripts lived in the session scratchpad and are described, not committed.

## Summary

The owner's six complaints trace to five root causes in how `create_world` builds a world, not to the reference picture alone.

1. **The terrain is synthesised from a pixel classification, not from geography.** A reference pixel colour becomes one of nine classes; each class gets a constant elevation (`reference-world.ts:21`) that is blurred, and a flat colour tint (`world.ts:1387`). There is no climate, no fertility, no ridge, no valley. Aenor is beige because the semantic reference marked 36% of its provinces as desert and nothing in the tool can say "fertile". Mountains are weak because every mountain pixel gets the same plateau height (peak 142 of 255; vanilla reaches 239).
2. **The colour and water outputs break the engine's own rules.** The engine overlays the colour map on the terrain textures at strength 0.75 (`pdxmap.shader:317`); vanilla colour maps are dark (mean value 74) and varied (1,084 distinct colours); ZIN's is bright (129) and has 14 distinct colours, so the map renders pale. Lakes are written at height 93 with graphical terrain index 14: the water shader makes a pixel at 93 only 56% opaque (`pdxwater.shader:281-282`), and index 14 points at the invalid texture 255 (`common/terrain/00_terrain.txt`). Vanilla lakes sit at 89 and use indices 0 or 15.
3. **Provinces, states, regions and countries ignore the land.** Provinces are a warped Voronoi diagram of random sites (`world-provinces.ts:109-171`), states and regions are breadth-first clusters (`world.ts:306-360, 926-972`), and the private politics script grew countries by centre-to-centre distance over that province graph. Measured against rivers and steep slopes, ZIN province borders are enriched 0.94 times over chance, state borders 0.69 and country borders 0.52 times; vanilla 1.37, 1.42 and 1.53 times. Locally straight country-border samples are 17.8% against vanilla's 9.3%.
4. **Rivers, straits and names are minimal.** Rivers are random-source, disjoint, single-pixel paths with no tributaries (`world-drainage.ts:228-292`): 0.33% of land pixels against vanilla's 2.28%, and dead straight on flat plateaus. `adjacencies.csv` and `adjacency_rules.txt` are written empty (`world.ts:1184-1190`). 257 of 275 strategic regions are called "Region N" and 941 of 1,332 states "District N".
5. **The world edge is not handled.** The engine wraps the map horizontally (V). The reference path never clears the edge columns, so land touches the seam on 1,037 rows (297 on the left, 740 on the right, none on both), and `ambient_object.txt` is written as a comment, which drops vanilla's north and south frame borders.

The plan has fifteen tool changes (C1 to C15), ordered by value for these complaints:

- **Phase 0, about 12 days, no new inputs:** real lakes (C1), appearance validators (C3), the edge and seam policy (C4), and a recalibrated colour map (C2, stage A). These alone make lakes visible, remove the washed look and the cut coasts.
- **Phase 1, about 37 days:** a typed `geography` input (C5), terrain synthesis with ridge profiles, volcanoes and plateaus (C6), erosion-aware rivers with names, tributaries and widths (C7), climate, biome and fertility driving terrain, trees, colour map, weather and development (C8), and engine-faithful composite renders (C14).
- **Phase 2, about 19 days:** feature-aware provinces, states and regions (C9), a border-naturalness metric and validator (C10), and a public territory-assignment service for country borders (C11).
- **Phase 3, about 11 days:** straits, passes and impassable ranges (C12); ports, supply, naval terrain, weather, names and development from geography (C13). C15 (generator versions, staged re-runs, performance) runs throughout, about 4 days.

Total about 83 engineer-days (roughly 17 weeks for one engineer, 9 to 10 with two), plus or minus 40%. Everything public stays offline, deterministic and behind the existing MCP surface; every new input is optional, and a `generator` selector keeps old requests byte-stable until the next major release.

The wrap cannot be removed by data (V); it can be made invisible by never letting land touch the seam. Recommended: an open-sea band at both vertical edges plus correct north and south frame borders; fallback: an impassable rim range with darkened colour map. Both need an owner-run in-game probe before the choice is final (see [Wrap](#the-horizontal-wrap)).

Method for the border rows: land-to-land border pixel edges (different province, state or owner on each side) are compared with a barrier set, namely pixels within 2 of a river pixel or within 1 of a height gradient of 2 or more per pixel. Enrichment is the share of border edges inside the barrier set divided by the share of all land pixels inside it (0.037 in ZIN, 0.407 in vanilla). Straightness takes every seventh border pixel and fits a line to the same-border pixels within 24 pixels; RMS under 0.9 counts as straight. The ZIN base rate is so low because the map has almost no barrier to align to, which is itself part of the finding.

### The horizontal wrap

Facts:

- The wiki states that the map loops horizontally (W), and the game's own Pacific crosses the seam.
- Every map sampler in the terrain, water and river shaders uses wrap addressing (`pdxmap.shader:14-158`, `pdxwater.shader:14-90`, `river.shader`), the engine has strings for `camera_quad_wrap`, `camera_quad_wrap_top`, `camera_quad_wrap_bottom` and a `CWrapWorldShadowMap` class, and day and night treats the map as a sphere: longitude is `fmod_loop((x - GMT_OFFSET) / MAP_SIZE_X + sun, 1)` and latitude spans `SOUTH_POLE_OFFSET` 0.17 to `NORTH_POLE_OFFSET` 0.93 (`standardfuncsgfx.fxh:347-355`, `00_graphics.lua:936-937`) (V).
- No define, `default.map` key or documented script switch turns the wrap off (the defines and engine strings were searched for wrap, loop, edge, pole and border keys) (V). A web search for third-party evidence found no Hearts of Iron IV specific source.
- Provinces at the first and last column are adjacent across the seam in the tool's builder and, by the wiki's X-crossing note, in the engine.

Conclusion: the wrap and the globe-style day and night cannot be removed with map data. A mod could in principle replace the shader files, which is outside this product and unverified here (U).

Recommended solution (A, open-sea band). Keep every land pixel at least `edge.bandPx` from column 0 and column width - 1, default `max(192, 4% of width)` (205 pixels at 5,120), make the band the deepest, darkest sea (the water colour map falls to a dark deep tint there), keep a small band at the top and bottom rows, and write the vanilla north and south frame borders at the right heights. The repeated copy then shows two bands (about 400 pixels) of empty ocean between the east and west coasts, instead of a coast cut off at a straight line. In the geography model the "lands that run off the edge" become lands that end in a coast, ice shelf or rim mountains inside the frame.

Fallback (B, rim wall). Where a land must reach the edge, end it in a rim range (height 150 to 230 over `rimPx`, default 160 pixels, with darkened colour map and permanent-snow terrain) and write `impassable` adjacencies for every province pair across the seam. The copy then shows a mountain wall meeting a mountain wall. This still shows repetition, but not a cut.

Not recommended: a vertical frame mesh at the seam (the north and south frames cannot be assumed to rotate or tile; U), and a shader override (U, outside the product).

Test before the choice is final (owner-run, never by the tool): build three synthetic 1,024 x 512 probes with the tool (no handling, sea band, rim), load each in the game, zoom to the maximum height, scroll across the seam at midday and at night, and compare screenshots. The tool never launches the game; the owner may publish approved screenshots under `docs/images/comparisons/`.

## Tool changes

Ordered by value for the owner's complaints and by dependency. "Public" means a general product feature that belongs in the tool. Every change keeps the MCP surface (no new CLI, dashboard or apply tool), uses the shared atomic rewrite, stays deterministic and bounded, and adds negative tests. Schema changes are additive and optional unless stated; the public tool schema and `schemas/*.json` are regenerated, and a review is recorded in the changelog. The next free ADR number is 0053.

### C1. Real lakes and water (phase 0)

- **Problem.** The Great Lake and every lake render as faint patches.
- **Root cause.** `world.ts:1245` writes 93 for lake pixels, the engine's water alpha there is 0.56 (V); `world.ts:1232` and `world-context.ts` give lakes the graphical index of terrain `lakes`, which is index 14 with the invalid texture 255; the shore has no bank; one flat water colour.
- **Design.**
  - Lake interior at height 89 (the sea value), a bank of at most 3 pixels rising 93 to 95, land from 96. Lakes in a state's neighbourhood keep their definition type `lake` and terrain `lakes`.
  - The graphical index for lake pixels is the index of `ocean` (15). `world-context.ts` excludes entries with `texture = 255` when choosing indices for a category and records the reason.
  - Water colour map: sea tint as a function of depth class (shelf bright, deep dark) calibrated to vanilla (mean (43,72,100), deviation (24,39,51)); lake tint (58,102,147) with a short shore gradient; the edge band of C4 is darkest. The three levels stay half, quarter and eighth size; DXT5 output is optional (see C15).
  - A lake needs at least 60 pixels unless named; smaller enclosed water becomes land or is reported.
  - Provide `waterAlpha(h)` as a shared function used by the validator and the renders (C14).
- **Schema.** None for the fix. The geography `lake` feature (C5) adds `depthClass` and `outflow`.
- **Validators and renders.** `MAP_LAKE_SURFACE_TOO_HIGH` (error, a lake pixel above 91 outside the bank), `MAP_LAKE_TEXTURE_UNRENDERED` (error, graphical index whose terrain entry has `texture = 255` on any pixel), `MAP_LAKE_TOO_SMALL` (warning), `MAP_WATER_COLORMAP_FLAT` (warning, deviation under 8). Render layer `water-alpha` (C14).
- **Tests.** Unit: a 256 x 256 fixture with one lake and one sea: all lake pixels 89 to 91 with bank, index 15, mean alpha at least 0.95; world-context test that `texture = 255` is never selected; determinism. Negative: injected 93 or index 14 yields the error; a 20-pixel unnamed lake is reported; a lake touching the sea is refused as a lake with the code `MAP_LAKE_CONNECTED_TO_SEA`.
- **ADR.** 0053 "Lake and water rendering conventions".
- **Effort.** 2 days. **Risks.** The in-game look is derived, not observed (U); keep the owner probe in the acceptance checks.

### C2. Calibrated colour map and graphical terrain (stage A in phase 0, stage B with C8)

- **Problem.** The map looks pale and flat; snow lands look like plains; Aenor looks like a desert.
- **Root cause.** `world.ts:1387-1416` ten flat tints at 1.45 to 1.75 times vanilla's brightness; no climate; only 12 graphical indices can be written; permanent snow tinted as plains.
- **Design, stage A (works on today's inputs).**
  - Per terrain class target colour tables from vanilla medians (plains (68,67,39), forest (47,55,24), hills (63,65,34), desert (118,98,64), mountain (59,60,34), marsh (51,54,29), jungle (36,51,16), snow (55,58,35)), each with a low-frequency variation of plus or minus 12% and a hillshade term from the normal map, so a class is never one colour.
  - Use more indices by rule: farmland (5) where moisture is high and slope low, sparse forest (4) at forest edges, `terrain_6` and `mountain_variation_grass` (20) above the tree line.
  - Class `snow` uses the permanent-snow indices 19 and 16 and their own colour map tint.
- **Design, stage B (with C8).** A solver instead of tables: choose for each pixel a target unlit albedo (biome palette, fertility, hillshade), look up the mean colour of the pixel's atlas tile (decoded at run time from the installed `atlas0.dds` with the existing DXT5 decoder `gui/dds.ts`, no asset copied), and invert the overlay of `pdxmap.shader:317` per channel by bisection (the overlay is monotone in the colour map value) to obtain the colour map value; report the clamp residual. This gives exact intent without trial and error. Tile means come from the installed game only; if the atlas is absent the stage falls back to a bundled table of the means listed above.
- **Schema.** `create_world.look: 'vanilla-temperate' | 'fertile' | 'arid' | 'custom'` selecting palette sets (C8 supplies per-biome overrides); `colormap: { profile: 'calibrated' | 'legacy' }`.
- **Validators and renders.** `MAP_COLORMAP_RANGE` (warning: land mean value outside 50 to 100, or fewer than 400 distinct 5-bit colours, or deviation under 15), `MAP_COLORMAP_SIZE` (error: not half the province map), `MAP_COLORMAP_ALPHA_CITY` (info). Composite render (C14) to review.
- **Tests.** Overlay inverse round trip within 1/255 where reachable; determinism; range tests; negative: a flat colour map triggers the warning; a mismatched size blocks the write.
- **ADR.** 0054 "Calibrated colour map and graphical terrain synthesis".
- **Effort.** Stage A 4 days (phase 0), stage B 3 days inside C8. **Risks.** The overlay model ignores lighting, fog and the snow layer, so it approximates; calibrate against owner screenshots (U).

### C3. Appearance-file validators (phase 0)

- **Problem.** "Offline validation passes" while terrain, rivers, trees, colour maps and normals are never read.
- **Root cause.** `validation.ts` reads definitions, provinces, states, regions, adjacencies, supply and positions only.
- **Design.** A new `map/appearance-validation.ts`, streamed in row strips under the existing budgets:
  - `rivers.bmp`: 8-bit, header colour count 0; only indices 0 to 11, 254, 255; no 2 x 2 block of river indices; orthogonal single-pixel paths; every connected river has exactly one index 0; indices 1 and 2 touch a river of a different branch; widths never decrease downstream when a downstream direction is known; no river pixel on sea; total river pixels at most 2.5% of land and 130,000 (performance guard, W).
  - `terrain.bmp`: size equals the province map; indices exist in `common/terrain`; none with `texture = 255` on land or water; water provinces under water indices.
  - `heightmap.bmp`: land at or above 96, sea at or below 94, lakes at or below 91 inside the interior; seam continuity (the two edge columns differ by at most 4).
  - `trees.bmp`: size 75/256 of the map; only known mesh indices; none over water.
  - colour maps: sizes and format, the C2 ranges, alpha.
  - `world_normal.bmp`: half size, unit-length decode within 2%, red/green response within 0.5 to 6 of the height step.
  - `cities.bmp`: indices defined in `cities.txt`; size.
- **Schema.** None; findings are diagnostics with source links, rolled up by the existing diagnostic limiter.
- **Codes.** `MAP_RIVER_*`, `MAP_TERRAIN_BITMAP_*`, `MAP_HEIGHT_*`, `MAP_TREES_*`, `MAP_COLORMAP_*`, `MAP_NORMAL_*`, `MAP_CITIES_*`.
- **Tests.** Golden bitmap fixtures with one defect each (2 x 2 river block, two sources, uphill, wrong size, bad index) and a clean fixture; bounded memory on a 13-million-pixel synthetic; vanilla-like fixture reports nothing blocking.
- **ADR.** 0053 covers the water part; add 0054 sections for the rest.
- **Effort.** 3 days. **Risks.** Too strict a rule on a vanilla-derived mod; keep soft rules as warnings.

### C4. World edge and seam policy (phase 0)

- **Problem.** Land is cut at the seam; the north and south frame borders are gone.
- **Root cause.** `world.ts:545-552` takes the reference mask unchanged (only `generateSurface`, `:255-259`, clears the edges); `world.ts:1192-1195` writes a comment as `ambient_object.txt`.
- **Design.**
  - `edge` option: `mode: 'sea-band' | 'rim' | 'none'`, `bandPx` (default `max(192, round(0.04 * width))` east and west), `polarPx` (default 64 north and south), `rimPx` (default 160). `none` keeps today's behaviour and raises the warning below. For geography worlds the default is `sea-band`.
  - `sea-band`: the surface is forced to deep sea inside the band after the lands are placed; features inside the band are an error that names them (no silent erasure). Water colour map darkens to the deepest tint at the seam.
  - `rim`: rim range and snow terrain inside `rimPx`, darkened colour map, and `impassable` adjacency rows for each pair of provinces across the seam.
  - `ambient_object.txt`: wind entity, the north frame at `z = height + 142`, the south frame at `z = -140`, the logo frame at `x = round(0.5327 * width)`, `z = height + 82` (vanilla's 2,190, -140 and (3,000, 2,130) for 2,048 by 5,632), through a flag `ambientFrame` (default on for geography worlds); ambient water objects are left out. The generated file passes the existing parser.
- **Schema.** `create_world.edge` object, `create_world.ambientFrame` boolean; both optional.
- **Validators and renders.** `MAP_EDGE_LAND_CUT` (warning: rows where land touches column 0 or width - 1; error when `edge.mode` is `sea-band`), `MAP_SEAM_LAND_ADJACENCY` (info: land-to-land seam pairs), `MAP_POLAR_LAND` (info). Render layer `seam`: three copies side by side (right strip, map, left strip) at bounded size.
- **Tests.** Seam validators on fixtures with land on 0, 1 and 2 sides; forced band leaves zero land in the band; a feature inside the band is refused; rim creates exactly the expected rows; `ambient_object.txt` parses and has the right z values for 2,560; determinism. Negative: `bandPx` over a quarter of the width is rejected.
- **ADR.** 0061 "World edge and seam policy".
- **Effort.** 3 days. **Risks.** The frame meshes at other widths (U); the owner may prefer to keep lands "running off the edge" (open question 2).

### C5. Geography model input (foundation)

- **Problem.** The tool accepts an image or noise; the geography model is a typed feature list (seas, lands, ranges, volcanoes, rivers, lakes, forests, deserts, marshes, ice, plains, straits, passes, sites) the tool cannot read.
- **Root cause.** `worldReferenceSchema` (`schemas/map.ts:55-117`) knows twelve semantic kinds as polygons and rivers as bare paths; no range, volcano, pass, strait, fertility or name has a place.
- **Design.** `create_world.geography` (inline) or `geographyPath` (a workspace-relative JSON, same canonical-path guard, snapshot and hash as `reference.imagePath`, at most 4 MiB), validated by a strict schema. All coordinates are normalised to the whole world frame, origin top-left, x east, y south, as `reference.labels` already are; lengths are fractions of the map width; heights are 0 to 1 of the relief scale. The features:

  | `range`   | id, name, path (points with optional `halfWidth`, `peak`), `profile` (`ridge`, `massif`, `escarpment`, `wall`), `asymmetry`, `snowline`, `spurs`, `barrier` (`none`, `impassable`, `fort-line`), `passes` |
  | `pass`    | id, `range`, `at` (0 to 1 along the range), `width`, `depth`, `fort` (level)                                                                                                                              |
  | `volcano` | id, name, at, `radius`, `peak`, `crater`, `active`                                                                                                                                                        |
  | `plateau` | id, polygon, `height`, `edge` (`cliff`, `ramp`)                                                                                                                                                           |
  | `river`   | id, name, `source` (point or lake id), `mouth` (point, sea, lake or edge), optional `via` points, `size` (1 to 5), `tributaries` (river ids), `delta`                                                     |
  | `lake`    | id, name, polygon or centre and radius, `depthClass`, `outflow` (river id), `gameplay` (`lake` or `inland-sea`)                                                                                           |
  | `biome`   | `forest`, `desert`, `marsh`, `ice`, `plains`, `farmland`, `jungle`, `steppe`, `tundra` as polygons with `density`, `fertility`, priority `z`                                                              |

- **Schema semver.** New optional fields on `create_world` plus the `generator` selector of C15: minor.

### C6. Terrain relief synthesis

- **Problem.** Mountains are blurred plateaus; ranges, valleys and volcanoes cannot exist.
- **Root cause.** One elevation constant per class (`reference-world.ts:21`), box blur, `96 + value * 110`.
- **Design.** Compute on a macro grid of half the map size (3.3 million cells at 5,120 x 2,560), then upsample bilinearly and add full-resolution detail in strips:
  1. Land and water from the lands and seas; coast roughness as seeded fractal displacement bounded by `coastDetail`; coast ramp land 94 to 98 over at most 6 pixels.
  2. Base relief: a low-frequency fractal (rolling plains 100 to 112), plateaus (`height` with cliff or ramp edge), a continental shelf term for the water colour map only (sea stays 89 as in vanilla).
  3. Ranges: for polyline `P` with arc parameter `t` and signed distance `d`, `u = d / halfWidth(t)`; cross profile `(1 - |u|^p)^q` with `p` 1.6 to 2.2 and `q` 1; along-range envelope from the control peaks times saddles at passes, `E(t) * (1 - depth * exp(-((t - t_pass) / w)^2))` (approximated with a polynomial); asymmetry scales `u` on one side; spurs are seeded side ridges at 50 to 70 degrees every 0.6 to 1.0 half-widths, length 0.5 to 0.8 half-widths, height 0.55 of the main crest; ridged multifractal detail `1 - |n|` squared, domain-warped by a low-frequency field and weighted by the profile, so detail exists only inside the footprint. Crest line and distance-to-crest are kept as rasters for C7, C9 and C10.
  4. Volcanoes: cone `base + (peak - base) * (1 - (r / R)^0.6)^1.3`, crater depression `-depth * (1 - (r / rc)^2)` inside `rc`, radial gullies as noise; volcanic terrain classes follow.
  5. Height targets in map values: lowland 96 to 112, rolling hills 112 to 135, highlands 135 to 160, mountains 160 to 215, great peaks 215 to 240, volcanoes up to 250; the snowline from the feature sets the permanent-snow indices. Percentiles of the result are checked against the vanilla distribution (median 107, 99th percentile 200 to 220).
  6. Normal map from the float field at half size with response -2.6 per height step (V), so shading matches vanilla's strength.
  7. Quantise to 8 bits only at the end, monotonically (no dither), after river carving (C7).
- **Schema.** Geography `range`, `volcano`, `plateau` (C5); `create_world.relief: { scale: 'vanilla' | 'low' | 'dramatic', detail: 0 to 1 }`.
- **Validators and renders.** `MAP_RELIEF_FLAT` (warning: 99th percentile under 150 on a map with ranges), `MAP_RANGE_CREST_LOW` (warning: crest under 90% of the requested peak outside passes), `MAP_PASS_NOT_LOWER` (error), `MAP_VOLCANO_NO_CRATER`. Renders `relief` (hillshade plus hypsometric tint) and `slope`.
- **Tests.** Determinism by hash; property tests: crest at least 0.9 times the peak except inside pass windows; the pass saddle is a local minimum along the crest and lies between its flanks; crater depressed; percentiles inside bounds; coast ramp monotone; normal-map response regression; negative: a range partly outside the frame, a range with fewer than 2 points, a pass off its range, overlapping volcanoes closer than their radii.
- **ADR.** 0056 "Terrain relief and hydrology synthesis" (with C7).
- **Effort.** 8 days. **Risks.** Aesthetic tuning; macro to full-resolution seams (test strip boundaries); 8-bit terracing on gentle slopes (check the normal map is computed from floats).

### C7. Hydrology: rivers from heights

- **Problem.** Rivers are sparse, straight, unnamed and never join.
- **Root cause.** `world-drainage.ts:198-292`.
- **Design.**
  1. Condition the macro heights with Priority-Flood plus a small slope epsilon (no flat cells, so no straight ties).
  2. D8 flow directions and flow accumulation weighted by a rainfall field from C8 (rain shadow). Lakes are sinks with outflow at the lowest rim; endorheic lakes allowed and reported.
  3. Named rivers: route as least-cost paths over the conditioned heights from source (or lake outlet) to the mouth, with a bias toward the given `via` points and a catchment raised along the route so it persists in the network. Unnamed rivers: the accumulation network above a threshold chosen to reach the target density (default 1.5 to 2.5% of land pixels, at most 130,000 pixels).
  4. Stream-power carving of the macro heights for a fixed number of steps (default 8) with `dh = k * Q^m * slope^n`, plus deposition that flattens river plains: this deepens valleys and produces floodplains, and the fertility field (C8) reads them.
  5. Rasterise to full resolution as orthogonal single-pixel paths (A* inside a corridor around the macro path on the full-resolution heights, with the existing chord-removal and no 2 x 2 blocks); width index from the catchment by log bins (3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11), never narrowing downstream; tributaries end in a red pixel (1) beside the main river; distributary starts use yellow (2); exactly one green source per connected river, on the main branch.
  6. Outputs for other stages: river raster with order, per-province crossing counts, named-river list with lengths (written to the artifact and, for names, to localisation under `WORLD_GEOGRAPHY_n`).
- **Schema.** Geography `river`, `lake.outflow`; `create_world.rivers` becomes a density target (default by land area) instead of a count, with the existing integer still accepted.
- **Validators and renders.** C3 rules plus `MAP_RIVER_UNREACHABLE_MOUTH` (error for a named river), `MAP_RIVER_STRAIGHT_RUN` (warning: a run longer than 40 pixels without a bend where the local slope allows), `MAP_RIVER_DENSITY` (warning outside 1% to 3%). Renders `rivers-order` and `river-network`.
- **Tests.** One-pixel and orthogonal property on random fixtures; every river ends in water, a lake or the edge; exactly one green per component; widths monotone; flow downhill on the conditioned field; the straight-run metric below the limit on a flat plateau fixture (regression for the black north); budget and determinism; negative: a named river whose mouth lies uphill and unreachable, a source in water, a river crossing the edge band, density request above the cap.
- **ADR.** 0056 with C6.
- **Effort.** 10 days. **Risks.** Runtime on 13 million pixels (target under 90 seconds for conditioning and accumulation on the macro grid); rasterisation corner cases.

### C8. Climate, biome, fertility, and the look they drive

- **Problem.** Aenor cannot be fertile; the look depends on class only; weather ignores geography.
- **Design.**
  - Temperature from a latitude profile plus a lapse rate with height; moisture from distance to water, prevailing wind and rain shadow (moisture falls on the leeward side of ranges by the integrated height gain along the wind direction), plus river proximity. Fertility from low slope, alluvial deposition, adequate moisture and temperature; named `farmland` and `forest` polygons override with their `fertility` and `density`.
  - A biome table maps (temperature, moisture, fertility, slope, height, overrides) to: province terrain type, a mix of graphical indices (with noise for natural transitions; farmland index 5 where fertility is high), tree class and density (sparse and dense forest, palm, jungle; target 8% to 12% of the tree grid inside forests, vanilla's 10%), and a colour-map target palette; snowline and ice give the permanent-snow indices and the 19 and 16 tints.
  - Colour map by the C2 stage B solver; the alpha channel carries city lights; `cities.bmp` writes style indices by region (the `cities.txt` group colour index) rather than only footprints.
  - `map/seasons.txt` written with the three bands tuned to the model's climate (band edges unverified, U; the file is added to the managed-output allow-list).
  - Strategic-region weather from the climate field per region (temperature range by month, rain, snow and `min_snow_level` by height) instead of the latitude formula (`world.ts:454-484`); sea regions take a naval terrain from depth class (`water_shallow_sea` on shelf, `water_fjords` for narrow inlets, `water_deep_ocean` deep).
- **Schema.** Geography `climate`, `biome` kinds; `create_world.look`; `seasons: 'vanilla' | 'generated'`.
- **Validators and renders.** `MAP_FERTILE_AREA_DESERT` (warning: a feature marked fertile with more than 10% desert terrain), `MAP_BIOME_UNMAPPED`, `MAP_COLORMAP_RESIDUAL` (info: share of pixels where the solver clamped), weather checks. Renders `biome`, `fertility`.
- **Tests.** Rain-shadow fixture (wet windward, dry lee); a fertile polygon yields at most 10% desert and at least 40% farmland and forest; tile-mean fallback table equals decoded means within 1%; seasons file parses; weather periods valid; determinism; negative: a biome overlap without priority, a `farmland` polygon over water, climate with out-of-range values.
- **ADR.** 0057 "Climate, biome and fertility fields".
- **Effort.** 8 days (includes C2 stage B). **Risks.** Looks need owner review; keep every table data-driven and versioned.

### C9. Feature-aware provinces, states and strategic regions

- **Problem.** Borders and shapes ignore rivers, ridges and shores; sizes are uniform.
- **Root cause.** `world-provinces.ts:109-171`; `world.ts:306-360, 905-972`.
- **Design.**
  1. Barrier field `B`: river pixels (weighted by width), crest and steep ridge pixels, lake shores, named feature boundaries (forest edge optional), range `wall` features.
  2. Target area field `A(x)` from development (fertility, coast, rivers), terrain and named sites: dense (80 to 250 pixels) in fertile, coastal and urban land; 250 to 450 in hills; 500 to 900 in mountains; 700 to 1,200 in desert and wasteland; a size distribution with a 10 to 1 spread like vanilla's (77 to 846). The map-scale rule is that area scales with the map's pixel count against vanilla's. Minimum 40 pixels (the tool floor is 20, the engine's is 8).
  3. Seeds by variable-radius Poisson-disc sampling (radius `sqrt(A / pi) * c`) with a hashed generator; one seed per site and per named city.
  4. Marker-controlled watershed: multi-source Dijkstra on the 8-neighbour grid with step cost `(1 + beta * B)` (beta about 12), so wavefronts meet on barrier lines. Ties broken by pixel index.
  5. Reuse the existing post-pass: disconnected-part split, sliver absorption, rebalance, four-corner repair, the bounding box limit (`world-provinces.ts:186-451`); a province that still straddles a major river is split along it.
  6. Sea provinces: seeds by depth class and named sea; cost favours shelf and coast following; open ocean cells 3,000 to 9,000 pixels with jittered, non-straight borders; one region per named sea where possible. Lakes: one province per lake (two when larger than 6,000 pixels).
  7. States: agglomerative clustering on the province graph inside each country (or inside the whole land when countries are not yet assigned), with edge affinity `shared border length * exp(-lambda * B) * similarity(biome)` and a balance term; stop at 6 to 12 provinces (median 8) and a population cap; hard cuts at major rivers, ranges and named regions; one strategic region and one country per state.
  8. Strategic regions: cluster states by basin and climate zone into 20 to 45 land provinces; sea regions by named sea, depth and strait.
- **Schema.** `create_world.partition`: `{ landArea: { fertile, hills, mountain, waste }, seaArea, provincesPerState: [min, max], regionSize: [min, max], barrierStrength, minProvincePixels }`; the three existing integer fields remain valid and map to defaults.
- **Validators.** Size distribution report; `MAP_PROVINCE_STRADDLES_RIVER` (warning), `MAP_STATE_SHAPE_ODD` (aspect over 4 or Polsby-Popper under 0.08), `MAP_PROVINCE_COUNT_HIGH` (warning above 14,000, error above 21,000, W), the C10 metric.
- **Tests.** Synthetic ridge and river fixtures: borders land on them (enrichment at least 2); full coverage; connectivity; no four-corners; bounding boxes within an eighth; sizes inside the requested bands; determinism by hash; scale test at 2,048 x 1,024 inside the time budget; the existing province tests stay green for `generator: 1`. Negative: contradictory size bands, a map too small for the minimum, more than the province cap.
- **ADR.** 0058 "Feature-aware province, state and region partition".
- **Effort.** 10 days. **Risks.** The hardest change to tune; four-corner and sliver repairs may fight the watershed; keep the legacy partition selectable.

### C10. Border-naturalness metric and validator

- **Problem.** No way to know a border is artificial except by looking.
- **Design.** `map/border-quality.ts`, computed for province, state, region and country borders on land. Barrier raster as in the measurement (river within 2 pixels, gradient of 2 or more within 1 pixel, lake shore, `wall` ranges; coast excluded). Reported per layer:
  - `alignment`: share of border pixel-edges in the barrier set, `enrichment` = alignment over the base rate; if the base rate is under 2% the metric is reported "not meaningful" rather than misleading.
  - `straightness`: fraction of samples (every 7th border pixel) whose 24-pixel window fits a line with RMS under 0.9 pixel.
  - `longRuns`: length share in Douglas-Peucker segments (tolerance 3 pixels) longer than 60 pixels.
  - `worstRuns`: the 20 longest artificial runs with coordinates, the two sides and length, so the politics step and agents can fix them.
  - Targets for an engineered world: enrichment at least 1.6 (vanilla 1.4 to 1.5, ZIN 0.5 to 0.9), straightness at most 0.12 (vanilla 0.093, ZIN 0.178). `MAP_BORDER_ARTIFICIAL_REVIEW` (warning) below either target, `MAP_BORDER_NOT_MEANINGFUL` (info) when the map has no barriers.
- **Schema.** `hoi4.map_inspect` returns `data.quality.borders`; a request flag `quality: true` (default on for full inspection); `create_world.borderQuality: 'report' | 'enforce'` (enforce blocks a write below target).
- **Renders.** Layer `border-quality`: border segments coloured by alignment, artificial runs highlighted.
- **Tests.** Synthetic borders: straight against river-following give enrichment at most 1 and at least 2, straightness at least 0.3 and at most 0.05; no-feature map reports "not meaningful"; determinism; budget on 13 million pixels; negative: a border layer with a single owner, a region with no land. Regression fixtures encode the ZIN numbers.
- **ADR.** 0059 "Border naturalness metric and validator".
- **Effort.** 4 days. **Risks.** The metric rewards alignment with rough height data; keep thresholds soft and show the numbers.

### C11. Territory assignment service (public) for natural country borders

- **Problem.** Country borders came from a private script that grew countries over province centres.
- **Design.** A new rewrite operation `assign_territory` (and `repartition_states`) on the shared core services:
  - Input: `countries` (tag, `seeds` as province ids, points or state ids, `weight`, `regions` as geography ids, `forced` and `blocked` provinces or ranges), `barriers: 'natural' | 'none'`, `rules` (`connectedMainland`, `islandPolicy: 'one-owner' | 'nearest'`), `smoothing` (passes, largest allowed share change 15%).
  - Algorithm: multi-source Dijkstra over the province graph with step cost `centroid distance * (1 + beta_b * barrierBetween + gamma * terrain change + delta * water) / weight`, forced seeds first; then connectivity repair and a deterministic local search that moves boundary provinces to the neighbour that reduces artificial border length (C10) within the size limit; one owner per small island.
  - Then states inside each country are re-clustered along natural lines with population, resource and building conservation (the existing split and merge distribution code), instead of splitting states along a border afterwards.
  - Country count limit raised to 512 for this operation (`create_world` keeps 200); one operation covers a whole map, so the 100-operation cap does not bind.
- **Schema.** New operation kind, new optional fields; `update_state` unchanged.
- **Tests.** Seeds grow into connected territories; weights reflect shares within 15%; the blocked barrier is never crossed; forced provinces stay; enrichment improves against the plain growth; conservation of population and resources (reusing `tests/unit/map-reorganise.test.ts` checks); determinism; negative: a seed in water, a country with no seed, two countries forcing one province, a blocked range that cuts a forced country off.
- **ADR.** 0060 "Territory assignment service".
- **Effort.** 5 days. **Risks.** Overlap with the ZIN politics script; keep country tables, anchors and lore rules in the ZIN repository.

### C12. Straits, passes, impassable ranges and adjacency rules

- **Problem.** No strait, no pass, no wall; adjacency files are empty.
- **Design.**
  - Straits: from geography `strait` features, and automatic detection of narrow channels (two land provinces on different landmasses with one sea province between and a gap of at most `narrowStraitPx`, default 24 pixels, about 170 km at 7.114 km per pixel) as an optional `autoStraits`. Rows `a;b;sea;through;-1;-1;-1;-1;RULE;name`; rules written to `adjacency_rules.txt` with named access (default: army and navy yes for friends, trade yes for neutral, army no for enemy), `required_provinces` the two land provinces and the icon on the through province. Island hops for archipelagos.
  - Ranges with `barrier: 'impassable'`: `impassable` rows for each pair of adjacent land provinces across the crest, except pairs whose shared border passes through a `pass` window (so passes are real gaps); a cap of 2,000 rows (vanilla 93); `fort-line` ranges instead add a land fort level to the pass provinces through state history.
  - Provinces wholly inside massifs may be set impassable states (`impassable = yes`, a state key vanilla uses) when the geography marks them; this is a separate, reviewed option.
  - Validators reuse the existing adjacency checks and add `MAP_STRAIT_NOT_NARROW`, `MAP_PASS_BLOCKED` (a pass window whose pair still has an impassable row), `MAP_RANGE_NOT_CLOSED` (a `wall` range with a gap not declared as a pass).
- **Schema.** Geography `strait`, `pass`, `range.barrier`; `create_world.autoStraits`.
- **Tests.** Fixture with two islands and a channel: row and rule emitted and valid under the existing checks; a range with two passes yields rows everywhere except the pass pairs; row cap enforced; adjacency terminator preserved; determinism. Negative: a strait between two points on the same landmass, a pass off its range, a wall with an undeclared gap.
- **ADR.** 0062 "Straits, passes and impassable ranges".
- **Effort.** 5 days. **Risks.** Engine interaction of impassable rows with supply and AI (U); test in an owner run.

### C13. Ports, supply, naval terrain, weather, names and development from geography

- **Problem.** Random ports, shortest-path railways, "Region N", "District N", random development.
- **Design.**
  - Ports: harbour score per coastal province from coast concavity (share of water within a radius), shelter behind islands, river mouths, depth class and nearby `site` kinds; ports at the best scores with levels from city rank; the rest of the existing rules (one per coastal territory) remain as a floor.
  - Supply and railways: step cost from slope, river crossing and range crossing (passes cheap), trunk lines between capitals and large cities, supply node in each capital state (existing rule); rails through passes by default.
  - Names: strategic region, sea region, state and victory-point names from geography `sea`, `land`, `range`, `river`, `site` features by containment and nearest, deterministic and unique; "District N" and "Region N" remain only as a counted fallback with warning `MAP_NAME_PLACEHOLDER` when more than 5% are placeholders.
  - Development: manpower, state category, infrastructure and victory points from fertility, river access, coast, passes and sites; resources from ranges and named `mine` sites (not random terrain rolls); keep conservation rules.
- **Schema.** `create_world.development: { model: 'geography' | 'legacy' }`; geography `site.kind`.
- **Tests.** Port ranking on a bay fixture; rail path prefers a pass over a ridge; name uniqueness and containment; development correlates with fertility (rank correlation over 0.6 on a fixture); conservation; determinism; negative: duplicate site ids, a site in water, a `mine` outside land.
- **ADR.** 0063 "Geography-driven ports, supply, names and development".
- **Effort.** 6 days. **Risks.** Economy balance is a ZIN design question; the tool produces a plausible default, the game design stays with the owner.

### C14. Composite renders and comparison evidence

- **Problem.** The preview shows ten class colours; the owner cannot judge colour, relief, lakes or the seam before the game.
- **Design.** A composite renderer, labelled "offline approximation, not a game screenshot":
  - Forward model: atlas tile mean colour per terrain index (installed `atlas0.dds`, decoded at run time, nothing copied), blended with neighbours, overlaid with the colour map by `GetOverlay(.., 0.75)` in gamma space, shaded by the normal map and the sun direction of the defines, snow where the permanent-snow indices apply, water by the shader's alpha formula over the water colour map, rivers, trees as dots, optional borders.
  - New layers on `hoi4.map_inspect`: `composite`, `relief`, `biome`, `fertility`, `slope`, `barriers`, `water-alpha`, `rivers-order`, `border-quality`, `seam`; existing `tile` and `area` arguments work with them; images stay under the render budget (at most 2,048 per side per image, content-addressed).
  - Comparison: a rewrite preview already returns reference, terrain and political panels; add a before-and-after pair (current mod against proposed) and a metrics table (heights, colour, rivers, lakes, borders, names) as `map-world-preview.json`; `compareTo` on inspect takes an artifact hash for two generations.
- **Tests.** Golden hashes of small renders; forward-model unit tests against the solver (round trip); layer determinism; budget refusal on oversize requests; negative: composite without an atlas falls back and says so; missing bitmap layers report which input was absent.
- **ADR.** 0064 "Composite map renders and comparison evidence".
- **Effort.** 6 days. **Risks.** The model approximates lighting and fog (U); never present it as the game.

### C15. Generator versions, staged re-runs, performance

- **Problem.** Changing the algorithms changes the world for the same seed, which `docs/map.md` promises will not happen; the owner needs to iterate on appearance without rebuilding provinces.
- **Design.**
  - `create_world.generator: 1 | 2` (default 1 until the next major release, then 2); version 1 is today's behaviour except the bug fixes C1, C3 and the optional `edge`, which are listed in the changelog.
  - Staged operation `regenerate_world_appearance` (layers: relief, rivers, terrain, trees, colour maps, normals, seasons): reads the existing land, sea and lake mask from `provinces.bmp` and refuses when the geography would change that mask, so provinces, states and politics survive an appearance iteration.
  - Managed-output allow-list adds `seasons.txt` and any new generated file; regeneration keeps the manifest rules (ADR 0052).
  - Performance contract at 5,120 x 2,560: relief, hydrology and climate on the half-size macro grid, strips for full-resolution work, at most 6 GiB heap and 6 minutes for the whole create operation, within the job worker's limits (8 GiB, 1,800 seconds); progress heartbeats (ADR 0042).
  - Optional DXT5 water maps (vanilla format) behind a flag if the Windows run shows a problem (U).
- **Schema.** New optional fields and one operation; no breaking change. A default flip is a major release.
- **Tests.** Version 1 hash regression for fixed seeds; staged re-run keeps `provinces.bmp` byte-equal and changes only appearance files; refusal when the mask would change; performance guard test on a 2,048 x 1,024 world; cancellation and stale-source tests.
- **ADR.** 0065 "Generator versions and deterministic output compatibility".
- **Effort.** 4 days. **Risks.** Two generators to maintain until 5.0.0.

## Acceptance checks

Tool checks (offline, in CI and in `hoi4.map_inspect`):

1. Lakes: every lake province pixel at or below 91 in the interior; no lake pixel with a `texture = 255` index; mean water alpha on lakes at least 0.95; the Great Lake renders as one body with the requested outflow.
2. Colour: land colour map mean value 50 to 100, at least 400 distinct 5-bit colours, solver residual under 5% clamped pixels; composite render colour within a mean error of 8 (CIE76) of the biome targets.
3. Relief: land median 100 to 115, 99th percentile 190 to 225; every `range` crest at least 0.9 of its requested peak except inside pass windows; every pass is a local minimum; volcano craters depressed; mean height step per half-resolution pixel at least 0.8.
4. Rivers: 1.5% to 2.5% of land pixels, at most 130,000; no 2 x 2 block, one green source per river; every named river present and reaching its mouth; no straight run over 40 pixels on sloping land; no river pixel on water.
5. Borders: province, state and country enrichment at least 1.6 and straightness at most 0.12; zero provinces straddling a named major river without a declared ford.
6. Shapes: land province area spread (p90 over p10) of at least 6; median provinces per state 6 to 10; states at most 4 aspect ratio.
7. Seam: zero land pixels inside the edge band; the seam render shows no cut coast; frame borders present at the right heights.
8. Straits and walls: every geography strait yields a valid row and rule; every wall range blocks all pairs except declared passes; row count under 2,000.
9. Names: placeholders at most 5% of states and strategic regions; sea regions named for every named sea.
10. Determinism and cost: same inputs give the same hashes on Windows and Linux CI; the full create operation at 5,120 x 2,560 within 6 minutes and 6 GiB; `npm run check` passes with the documented unit, schema, security and stdio smoke tests for every new field.

Owner checks (never by the tool): the lake and edge probe maps; one screenshot per named lake, range, volcano and the Great Wall; a night view for the seam; a start as Aenor, Volgan, Zol, Lerendor and the Magical Forest (the ZIN acceptance list) after each map rebuild; the owner may publish approved screenshots with attribution under `docs/images/comparisons/`.

## Risks

| Risk                                                                              | Effect                                     | Mitigation                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

## Open questions and proposals

1. **Great Lake: a decorative lake or a navigable inland sea?** The lore has ports on the Great Lake. A `lake` province may not host fleets or ports (U). Proposal: model it as an inland sea (`lake.gameplay: 'inland-sea'`, sea provinces plus a strategic region, ports on its shore) reachable only by a rule-controlled canal adjacency, and keep small lakes as `lake` provinces. Settle with the probe map.
2. **How should "lands that run off the edge" be treated?** Proposal: sea band (A) with the lands ending in coasts, ice shelves or rim mountains inside the frame; fallback rim wall (B). The owner decides after the probe.
3. **Map size.** Proposal: keep 5,120 x 2,560 (13.1 million pixels, the cap is 13,238,272). A wider map needs a lower height; 6,144 x 2,048 is the largest at that aspect ratio.
4. **Province density.** Proposal: about 9,000 to 11,000 provinces on 5,120 x 2,560 (vanilla 13,413 on 11.5 million pixels), land median about 200 pixels, sea mean 2,500 to 4,000; the current 7,108 is too coarse for natural borders.
5. **Custom terrain textures.** The atlas has 16 tiles; an Erdae look (golden farmland, volcanic ash, ice shelf) may need new tiles. Proposal: a later `terrain atlas builder` (generated tiles in, DXT5 with mips out) if the owner wants it; the owner's rule is that all art is image-generated, which the builder would respect.
6. **City styles.** `cities.txt` groups are western by default. Proposal: write regional group indices into `cities.bmp` and leave style meshes to the art track.
7. **Seasons and sea ice latitude.** Sea ice only appears above 0.74 of the map height and seasonal tree colour above 0.5 (V). Proposal: put the cold lands and the frozen seas in the north quarter in the geography model, and treat the rest as temperate; test the season bands with the probe.
8. **Names.** Which source supplies the many state and region names the model does not name? Proposal: geography `site` and `land` names first, the owner's `name_table.csv` second, generated placeholders last with the 5% warning.
9. **The Great Wall.** Impassable with named gates, or a fort line with open crossings? Proposal: `barrier: 'impassable'` along the wall with four to six `pass` gates carrying level-5 forts, which matches "a hard border between Middle Lands and Magical Lands" and the key-sites spec.
10. **Windows confirmation.** The Linux runs accepted uncompressed water colour maps; the Windows build has not been tested with them. Proposal: owner run of the first phase 0 build and read the error log with `hoi4.error_log`.
