# ADR 0060: Territory assignment service

## Context

Country borders of a generated total-conversion map came from a private script that grew countries over province centres and then cut states along the border, so states fell to 4.7 provinces and the borders ignored rivers and ranges (country enrichment 0.50 against 1.46 in the base game, ADR 0059). The feature-aware partition (ADR 0058) exports a weighted province graph and the border metric (ADR 0059) can score any grouping of provinces without reading the raster again, so the pieces for a public service existed. A politics pass also needs more than the `create_world` limits (200 countries) and more than 100 operations of one state each (a pass over more than a thousand states took 14 calls with a full validation each). Plan section C11 asks for one rewrite operation that covers a whole map.

## Decision

Three operations on `hoi4.map_rewrite`, all planned by the shared map core (`territory*.ts`, `state-restructure.ts`), with stale-source checks, preview, atomic replacement and recovery unchanged.

**`assign_territory`.** Up to 512 countries, each with a tag, a weight, seeds (`{ state }`, `{ province }` or a pixel `{ x, y }`; the first is the capital), forced and blocked selections (states, provinces, strategic regions), preferred strategic regions, an optional `overlord` (a subject is grown inside its overlord's territory) and `mergeInto` (the territory is relabelled to another listed country, the old tag stays a core), and extra cores and claims. The unit is `state` (whole states; ownership, controller, cores and claims are edited) or `province` (provinces are assigned and the states a border cuts are regrouped). `scope` limits the states that take part, `unowned` marks land nobody owns, and `rules` set `connectedMainland` and `islandPolicy` (`one-owner`, `nearest`, `unowned`).

The graph is the border edge table of the metric turned into a province graph (`buildTerritoryProvinceGraph`): every shared border has its length, the mean strength of the natural features on it (river and wall 1, steep slope 0.8, terrain change 0.45, lake shore 0.35) and its natural length (barrier features count fully, terrain changes half). States are aggregated into atoms with the same two numbers. `provinceEdgeCosts` of ADR 0058 gives the step cost, `centroid distance * (1 + 6 * barrier)`, with seeded jitter of up to 8% so that featureless borders are not straight bisectors.

The algorithm (`growTerritory`, no `Math.pow` or `Math.hypot`, so it is the same on every platform):

1. A multi-source Dijkstra grows all countries at once. A country's multiplier divides its step cost. Landmasses without a seed are reached over sea edges to the three nearest atoms elsewhere, at three times the distance; `one-owner` gives the whole island to the first country that reaches it, `nearest` lets every atom go to its nearest owner.
2. The multipliers are corrected with `sqrt(target / achieved)` for up to 20 rounds and the best round (smallest size error, then shortest artificial border) is kept. The target of a country is its weight share of the assignable land, corrected by water filling: a country alone on a landmass cannot have less than the landmass, and none can have more than the landmasses it starts on and the islands.
3. A country whose seeds on its capital's landmass fall into several pieces is joined by a forced corridor (the cheapest path through atoms that are not forced to another country); a seed that blocked or forced land cuts off is refused before growth (`MAP_TERRITORY_FORCED_UNREACHABLE`).
4. A deterministic local search (up to 20 passes, 4 by default) moves a boundary atom to the neighbouring country when that shortens the artificial border length, if the donor stays connected, no seed or forced atom moves, the size error does not leave the tolerance (15%) and a country changes by at most 15%.

Subjects form a tree: a bloc (an overlord with its subjects' seeds and weights) is grown as one country first and then split by the same algorithm inside its territory.

In the province unit every state is split by owner, fragments below `stateSize.min` provinces join the neighbour of the same owner and strategic region that shares the longest border (without exceeding `max`), groups equal to an old state only change ownership, and the rest go to the regrouping engine.

**`repartition_states`.** For the states of the selected owners (or all), provinces of one owner and strategic region are clustered along natural lines (`clusterProvinces` of ADR 0058) into states of `provincesPerState` `min`, `target` and `max`, then regrouped by the same engine. Clusters equal to an existing state are left untouched.

**Regrouping engine (`restructureStates`).** Given complete groups of provinces, the group that overlaps an old state most keeps its identifier, file and history; others become new states in `<id>-AGENT_STATE.txt` named `STATE_<id>` with an English name derived from the state they were cut from; old states no group inherits are removed. Manpower, resources and slot buildings are shared by land pixels with the largest-remainder rule (every total is conserved exactly; decimals in hundredths), levels keep the highest value, victory points and province buildings follow their provinces, cores and claims are the union of the contributing states. A group must lie in one strategic region. Dated history that would be lost or rewritten (`MAP_DATED_STATE_HISTORY_CONFLICT`), states without geometry and malformed ownership refuse the whole operation. Building positions are re-pointed in one pass and `remapReferences` rewrites script references through the existing remap engine. The engine builds the workspace index once, so a regrouping of hundreds of states costs one validation, not one per state.

**`update_states`.** A list of up to 5,000 `{ stateId, changes }` entries, each with the changes of `update_state`; one index, all or nothing, a state at most once.

**Border quality.** `borderQuality` (`off`, `report`, `enforce`; default `report` on both territory operations) measures the proposed map after every operation of the request with the metric of ADR 0059: the country and state layers for `assign_territory`, the state layer for `repartition_states`. `enforce` turns a miss of the targets (or `borderTargets`) into the blocker `MAP_TERRITORY_BORDER_ARTIFICIAL` that names the layer, the failing metrics and the longest artificial run. The same option on `create_world` (G-58) measures all four layers of the new world (`MAP_BORDER_QUALITY_MEASURED`, `MAP_WORLD_BORDER_ARTIFICIAL`) and refuses `enforce` with the legacy partition, which cuts at chance level (`MAP_BORDER_QUALITY_ENFORCE_NEEDS_FEATURE_AWARE`). Results carry `data.territory` (sizes against targets, the border score, the same seeds grown on distance alone, and counts of states changed, created and removed) and the artifact `map-territory-report.json` holds everything, including the complete metric report. The border edge table builder became a generator (`buildBorderEdgeTableSteps`) so the synchronous planner can run it; the async function drives it.

**World generation.** A feature-aware `create_world` without a reference picture now grows its countries with `growTerritory` over the partition graph instead of blind breadth-first growth; legacy output is byte-identical.

## Consequences

Measured on project-owned synthetic fixtures (`tests/helpers/map-territory-fixture.ts`): on a 1,024 x 512 partitioned world with rivers, ridges and a lake and six countries of unequal weights, the natural-line enrichment of country borders is 3.1 to 3.6 against 1.2 to 2.5 for the same seeds grown on distance alone (natural share 0.77 to 0.89 against 0.29 to 0.61); smoothing alone raises enrichment from 2.6 to 3.2 on provinces. On a generated 768 x 512 world with five countries the enrichment is 1.7 to 2.1 against 1.1 to 1.9. Once, outside the fixtures, on a generated total-conversion map (13.1 million pixels, about 1,500 states and 15,000 provinces), about 300 countries were assigned in 15 s by state (enrichment 4.3 against 2.9 on distance alone) and in 26 s by province with several hundred new states (3.7 against 1.6), and a repartition took 23 s; peak resident memory was 2.3 GiB with the generated world held in memory.

Sizes are met within the tolerance only when the atoms are fine enough: with whole states and a few states per country the error is bounded by the size of a state, and countries on small islands are bounded by the island. The statistics report the error against the feasible target and `MAP_TERRITORY_SHARE_OFF` says so. Unsupported constructs, stated precisely:

- Strategic regions are the region input of `regions`, `forced`, `blocked` and `scope`; geography model region ids are not accepted yet (G-59).
- Provinces in no state cannot be owned: a seed, forced or selected province outside every state is refused with `MAP_TERRITORY_PROVINCE_WITHOUT_STATE`, and the province unit leaves such provinces out (G-61).
- States whose dated history would be lost or rewritten are refused (`MAP_DATED_STATE_HISTORY_CONFLICT`) rather than rewritten.
- The tag pattern is three capital letters or digits; tags are not checked against `common/country_tags`.

Unowned land: atoms in `unowned`, and landmasses without a seed under `islandPolicy: "unowned"`, take part in no growth. With `ownership.unowned: "clear"` (the default) their owner and, when `controller` is `owner`, their controller are removed; `"keep"` leaves their ownership as it was. States outside `scope` are not edited. A seed or forced selection inside unowned or out-of-scope land is refused with `MAP_TERRITORY_SEED_UNAVAILABLE`. Refusals of the growth name the state (unit `state`) or province (unit `province`) of the request, never the internal atom index.
