# ADR 0075: Focus layout quality is a verdict, measured against the game's own trees

## Context

Agent-made focus trees passed every structural check and still looked wrong: the main line stepped left by a column on every row, one branch floated in a corner, large parts of the canvas were empty, and seven focuses in a row offered no choice. Five causes sat in the tools:

- The sibling symmetry and anchor checks ran only for focuses placed by the solver (`auto`), so a tree read back from a file with fixed or relative coordinates reported no cohorts at all.
- The only global balance measure was the centring of the bounding box, which a lopsided tree passes when its two outermost leaves cancel.
- Crossings and connector-through-focus were counted on the cubic curves of the icon render. The game draws right-angle connectors. In a study of 54 vanilla trees the curved model reported 766 crossings against 86 in a right-angle model, and it counted every connector inside an all-to-all bundle against the rest.
- The documented repair, `layoutMode: "compact"`, demanded zero curved crossings, so it refused every real tree it was tried on (four vanilla trees and two mod trees), after 21 to 70 seconds, with no remedy in the refusal.
- Renders were linked, not shown, and `status: ok` with `no blocking diagnostics` read as "done".

Nothing set a limit in numbers. The kit's rules were "compact, symmetrical, centered".

## Decision

**A layout verdict beside the structural answer.** `hoi4.focus_inspect`, `hoi4.focus_render` and `hoi4.focus_rewrite` return `layoutQuality`: a verdict (`pass`, `review`, `revise`, `blocked`), a 0 to 100 score for before and after comparisons (the verdict is authoritative), a scorecard of up to 12 rows with a limit, the vanilla median and a fix for each failing row, `nextActions` and `notEvaluated`. `validation.passed` keeps its structural meaning; a second check, `focus-layout-verdict`, carries the layout one, and the structural message now says it is not a layout verdict. A diagnostic `FOCUS_LAYOUT_VERDICT_<VERDICT>` puts the verdict into the capped diagnostic list. `hoi4.focus_rewrite` and `previous` on `hoi4.focus_inspect` return a `layoutDelta` with moved focuses, a hash of the unmoved ones and hashes of prerequisites and exclusions.

**A rubric of three classes.** Hard defects H1 to H5, soft metrics S1 to S11, and design flags D1 to D4 (`focus/quality/metrics.ts`). Every metric is computed on resolved coordinates whatever the position mode, and is invariant under translation, row shift and mirroring (tests). H1 to H3 are `blocked`. H4, H5 and S9 depend on the connector model below and raise a tree to `review` at most. S8 is shown and never judged. Design flags always go to a person.

**Limits come from the installed game.** `focus-quality-calibration.json` holds the metrics of the 67 national focus trees of 8 or more focuses, in the buckets 40 to 120 and over 120 focuses (2 smaller trees are kept but borrow the medium limits; a tree below 40 focuses is never asked to `revise`). Only derived numbers and the SHA-256 of each vanilla file are stored. `scripts/focus-quality-calibration.ts` recomputes it, and the local test `tests/local/focus-quality-calibration.test.ts` fails when it differs from the installed game. Trees with more than one coordinate for a focus the tool cannot evaluate (an `offset` block, a conditional branch, an anchor in another tree) are marked `ambiguousPositions`: their overlaps are not defects, and they stay out of the connector statistics.

**The verdict rules are data.** The first rules, taken from the research (`review` beyond the 90th percentile, `revise` beyond the vanilla maximum or with three metrics beyond the 90th percentile), flagged too much: with twelve calibrated metrics a vanilla tree leaves a limit by chance, so 72 percent of the vanilla trees were at `review` and, in leave-one-out testing, 26 percent of an unseen vanilla-quality tree were at `revise`. The stored rules are `review` beyond the vanilla range (maximum, or minimum for the fill ratio), `revise` beyond that range plus a margin of (maximum minus median), or with five soft metrics beyond the range. With limits fitted without the tested tree (`npm run focus:calibrate -- --report`, 65 trees of 40 or more focuses):

| Test                               | pass   | review | revise |
| ---------------------------------- | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| In sample                          | 100.0% | 0.0%   | 0.0%   |
| Leave one out                      | 81.5%  | 15.4%  | 3.1%   |
| Fit on one half, test on the other | 60.0%  | 32.3%  | 7.7%   |

The research targeted at most 10 percent `review` and 3 percent `revise` on held-out trees. Leave one out meets the second and misses the first; the half and half split, which fits on about 16 trees per bucket, misses both. Vanilla is not flawless and 65 trees are few, so these rates are a floor on how often a clean tree is flagged, not a measure of agent trees. The human ratings of 15 to 20 trees that the research asks for are not collected yet.

**An in-game connector model.** Connectors are right-angle (stem, a bar in the gap above the child's row, stem), a plaque is 1.6 columns by half a row, and connectors inside an all-to-all bundle or an OR group are never counted against each other. This model was read from one screenshot of adjacent rows; multi-row bar placement is not verified in the game, so every number from it is advisory until the maintainer confirms it with screenshots of a multi-row connector, two focuses one and two columns apart, an exclusive pair and an OR group. The curved-connector diagnostics are dropped from the inline results (the linked artifact keeps them) and the layout solver itself still optimises the curved model; replacing it belongs to the Rust layout engine.

**A compact gate that compares with what is in place.** A candidate is accepted when the old gate accepts it, or when it is no worse than the current layout in the right-angle model: no more hard layout diagnostics (the curved ones excluded), same-row spacing violations, crossings or connector-through-plaque counts, a centred canvas unless the tree already was not, and no worse layout verdict. A new tree starts from zero. When the layout already has the verdict `pass`, a whole-tree reflow is skipped; when no candidate beats the current layout, or the best one is worse on something protected, the call returns the plan unchanged with `compactOutcome` (`verdict_pass`, `no_better_candidate`, `candidates_regress`, `blockedBy`) and the diagnostic `FOCUS_COMPACT_NOTHING_TO_IMPROVE`. A refusal of a new tree lists each blocking item with its values and a remedy. An explicit `compactFocusIds`, `pinnedFocusIds` or `symmetryGroups` request is always searched.

On the six real trees that were refused (vanilla Estonia, Iceland, USA and Finland, and two mod trees), the new gate returns `nothing_to_improve` for all of them: the four vanilla trees at once because they already pass (the search used to take 21 to 70 seconds and was refused), the two mod trees after the search because every candidate regresses `connectorCrossingCount`, `connectorNodeIntersections` or the layout verdict (one of them is `blocked` by plaque overlaps that the search cannot fix without adding crossings). The search itself is not faster; that belongs to the Rust layout engine.

**Pictures in the result.** `hoi4.focus_render` returns an in-game-style schematic as inline images: an overview of at most 1,500 pixels and up to five close-ups of the places the scorecard blames or that `cropBranchIds` (new) and `cropFocusIds` name. They are drawn with the same geometry the metrics use and carry the grid coordinates; they are tool renders without icons. Images are exempt from the 32 KiB structured-result budget and have their own ceiling of 3,000,000 base64 characters; branches are the declared `branchId` or, when none is declared, the subtrees below the first fork of each root.

**Words the agent reads.** The three tool descriptions state the workflow and what each verdict means (within the 512-byte budget), the server instruction adds one sentence for focus trees, the plan schema describes `position.mode`, `preferredX`, `preferredY`, `branchId` and `laneId`, and `docs/focus.md` has the workflow, the verdict table and the metric table.

## Consequences

An agent that stops at `status: ok` can no longer read it as "done": the same response says `revise` and names the rows. Trees with hand-typed coordinates that vanilla's own trees would also exceed (`review`) are not blocked, and trees that are far outside the vanilla range are. Because the factory gates commits on `blocked` and `revise` with a recorded override, the false-revise rate above is the rate of overrides to expect for vanilla-quality trees.

Not covered: placement by the solver per branch lane and corridor insertion of new branches (the Rust layout engine); `offset` blocks and their triggers (focuses with one are excluded, not evaluated); the supplied Soviet reconstruction as a fixture (it needs the maintainer's consent to publish); the kit skill text; per-branch scoring (branches are cropped and summarised, the verdict is for the whole tree); human calibration; and a check against the game of the connector model.

Tests: hand-computed vectors for every metric on synthetic trees, invariance under translation, mirroring, row shift and input order, the bundle and OR-group exemptions, the conditional-branch and offset exemptions, the verdict rules including the size cut-offs and the advisory metrics, the delta hashes, the calibration file's self-consistency, the recomputation against the installed game (local), the compact gate on bundle fixtures with a new-tree refusal that names its remedy, and the three tools through an MCP client (inline images are PNGs, the verdict reaches the capped lists, many trees stay inside the budget, nothing-to-improve leaves the source alone).

## Amendment (2026-11-02): the connector bar runs under the parent

The in-game run of 2026-11-02 on 1.19.4.1, at the default UI scale and zoom (1 column = 96 px, 1 row = 130 px, a plaque centred on its focus's coordinates), showed where the game draws connectors. A plaque is about 1.61 columns wide and 0.28 rows high, and icon and plaque together span about 0.8 rows with the icon above. A connector from a parent on row py to a child on a lower row leaves the parent plaque's bottom centre in a short stem, runs along a bar at about py + 0.28 rows (just under the parent, not a quarter row above the child), and drops in the child's column into the top of the child's icon. All-to-all bundles and OR groups share one dotted bar at that height, with one vertical per child (an OR group's vertical leaves the bar's midpoint). The exclusive mark sits midway between the two focuses on the plaque line and fits at a spacing of 2. For adjacent rows the earlier model nearly agrees; for multi-row connectors the long vertical runs in the child's column instead of the parent's.

The model in `orthogonal.ts` and the Rust engine's `geometry.rs` now follow these numbers: bars are grouped by the parent's row, a crossing is a child's vertical passing strictly inside a bar under a row strictly between its parent and child, and a connector through a plaque is that vertical within 0.805 columns of another focus on such a row. The Rust engine compares in integer hundredths of a row and of a column, so 0.14, 0.28, 0.66 and 1.61 are exact. A shared vector of hand-counted trees (`engine/crates/focus/tests/fixtures/connector-parity.json`) is checked by both implementations and keeps cases where the earlier model counted differently. Other UI scales and zoom levels are unverified, and H4, H5 and S9 stay advisory under the verdict rules.

Recomputed on 1.19.4.1, vanilla trees almost never put a vertical through a plaque or across a bar under the corrected model. The H4 limits fell from 6.6038 and 11.4578 to 1.0101 and 2.0202 (40 to 120 focuses) and from 6.1611 and 10.4265 to 0.4202 and 0.8404 (over 120), H5 from 2.4 and 4.8 to 0 and 0.02 and from 7.7982 and 15.5964 to 0.5319 and 1.0638, and S9 moved from 7.4627 and 14.9254 to 11.9403 and 23.8806 and from 2.8571 and 4.6504 to 1.5584 and 3.1168 (review and revise limits per 100 links). Leave one out now gives 80.0 percent `pass`, 16.9 percent `review` and 3.1 percent `revise`, and the half and half split 55.4, 36.9 and 7.7 percent.
