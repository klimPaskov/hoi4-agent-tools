# ADR 0062: Straits, passes, impassable ranges and inland seas

## Context

`create_world` wrote `adjacencies.csv` with only a terminator row and `adjacency_rules.txt` as a comment. A strait, a canal, an island hop or a wall could not exist, so a geography model's walls and named channels blocked nothing. The base game has 151 `sea` rows (144 of them with a through province that touches both ends, 148 land to land, 3 sea to sea across a land province, a gap between the two provinces of at most 30 pixels) and 93 `impassable` rows (92 between touching land provinces). Named crossings with rules, walls with gates, and a way to put ports on an inland sea were needed.

## Decision

For a world built from a geography model (and only then) the generator plans the rows and rules in `src/hoi4_agent_tools/map/gameplay/crossings.ts`, from the finished province raster and the resolved model. Nothing is written for a world without a geography; the ranges, passes and straits of the model drive everything.

**Straits.** Every model `strait` becomes a `sea` row `from;to;sea;through;start_x;start_y;stop_x;stop_y;RULE;name` and a rule in `adjacency_rules.txt` (name from the strait id, `required_provinces` the two ends and the through province, `icon` the through province, or the first end for a canal). The probe line is the strait's `path`, else the line between the nearest vertices of the two lands (or the land and the point), run 14 pixels further inland at both ends. The provinces along it form runs of one kind; a run of water between two land runs is land to land, a run of land between two water runs is sea to sea (a canal). The through province is the province of the run that touches both shores, else one shore (narrow channels the partition cut in two reach only one, as 6 of the base game's 151 rows do). The shores' closest pair of pixels gives the start and stop coordinates (y counted from the bottom, as building positions are; plausibility checked against Panama and Kiel in the base game). The strait's `rule` sets the access: `open` (friend and neutral may use it; enemy and contested keep submarines and block armies, ships and trade, like the base game's Gibraltar), `army-closed` (no army in any state) and `navy-closed` (no ship or submarine). A strait is reported `MAP_STRAIT_NOT_NARROW` when its shores are more than 48 pixels apart (or 1.5 times its declared width), `MAP_STRAIT_UNRESOLVED` when no run of provinces separates two shores, and `MAP_STRAIT_DUPLICATE` when an earlier row has the same two provinces. `create_world.autoStraits` (true or `{ maxGapPx }`, default 24) also writes a rule-less row for every sea province that separates two landmasses by no more than that gap (at most 400, narrowest first).

**Two coasts of one land.** A strait whose two declared shore points lie on one land and whose straight line crosses water (a bay mouth, a neck of sea) is a crossing between two land provinces of that land: the sea province nearest the strait's middle that touches a province of the land near each end, where the two do not touch each other. It is reported as `MAP_GEOGRAPHY_STRAIT_SAME_LAND` with severity `info`. Two points of one land across no water, or a strait whose ends were derived from a path alone, stay an error or a warning. `bay` joins `ocean`, `sea`, `gulf` and `fjord` as a sea kind; the generator treats it like `gulf`, and it is kept for later naming stages.

**Walls and passes.** A range with `barrier: 'impassable'` blocks every pair of land provinces whose shared border crosses the range's path: border pixel pairs within a band of the path (6 pixels for a wall, 40% of the half-width up to 40 for other profiles) whose two sides lie on opposite sides of the path count; a pair is blocked when at least 2 pairs and at least 20% of its border do. A pair whose border passes through a pass window (the pass width times 0.75 plus 4 pixels each side of its position) is a gate and has no row, so a pass is real. Rows are `from;to;impassable;-1;-1;-1;-1;-1;;range name`, sorted, with a cap of 2,000 (`MAP_IMPASSABLE_ROW_LIMIT`, refused rather than truncated). A pass with `fort` above 0 puts `bunker = level` on both provinces of its best gate pair in the state history. A `fort-line` range writes no rows, only the pass forts. The generator now gives the partition the regions, named seas and walls of the model (`partitionFieldsFromGeography`), so provinces do not straddle a wall; where they still do, `MAP_RANGE_NOT_CLOSED` (warning) lists the undeclared gaps along the wall, and `MAP_PASS_BLOCKED` (warning) names a pass whose gap lies over water or over a single province. Whole provinces inside a massif are not made impassable states (`impassable = yes` is a state key the base game uses); that stays a separate reviewed option.

**Inland seas.** A lake with `gameplay: 'inland-sea'` is written as sea provinces after the relief is synthesised (heights, bank and rivers are unchanged), because the engine gives ships and naval bases only to sea provinces; it is partitioned, ported, named and put into a strategic region like any sea. If the sea touches the open sea nothing more is needed. Otherwise a canal row (`<lake>_canal`, rule open, through the land province that touches the inland sea, or the first of two land provinces) joins it to the nearest ocean province, unless a declared strait already does (`MAP_INLAND_SEA_CONNECTED`); an inland sea more than two land provinces from the ocean stays sealed (`MAP_INLAND_SEA_SEALED`). Other lakes stay `lake` provinces and cannot hold a port.

**Validators on any map** (`gameplay/crossing-validation.ts`, run by `hoi4.map_inspect` and by every world being created): `MAP_ADJACENCY_RULE_MISSING` and `MAP_ADJACENCY_RULE_DUPLICATE` (errors), `MAP_ADJACENCY_RULE_INVALID` (error: fewer than two `required_provinces`, an unknown province, no name), `MAP_ADJACENCY_RULE_UNUSED` (info), `MAP_IMPASSABLE_NOT_ADJACENT` (a warning from three rows and 5%, else info; the base game has one), `MAP_IMPASSABLE_NOT_LAND`, `MAP_ADJACENCY_ROW_LIMIT` (more than 2,000 rows), `MAP_STRAIT_NOT_NARROW` (a land crossing between provinces more than 48 pixels apart), `MAP_RAILWAY_CROSSES_IMPASSABLE` and `MAP_UNIT_POSITION_MISSING` (a warning above 1% of the provinces, else info; the base game lacks 7). The base game raises nothing above information.

## Consequences

Old requests and reference-only worlds are unchanged. The through province of a crossing cut by the narrow-strait rule touches only one shore; the engine's behaviour for that case is the base game's own six rows, not otherwise verified here (U), and how `impassable` rows interact with supply, railways and the AI is also unobserved (plan, unverified facts). Gates are as coarse as the provinces: a 31-pixel gate frees the one or two province pairs whose border passes through it. A pair is blocked or free as a whole, so a wall segment is only as straight as the province borders along it. Strait line coordinates follow the model's polygons, which the generated coast leaves by up to the coast displacement; the through province is chosen on the generated map, not on the polygon.

Regressions: rows and rules against an independent read of the provinces bitmap (touching land pairs, through province kind, rule text and access), the wall closing the continent when gates are removed from the graph and opening when they are present, forts on both gate provinces, the row cap, an unclosed wall under the legacy partition, a gate over water, duplicate and unresolvable straits, the validators on edited rows and rules, and the installed game raising nothing above information.
