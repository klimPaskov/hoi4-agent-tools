# ADR 0056: Terrain relief and hydrology synthesis

## Context

`create_world` turned a picture into terrain by giving every pixel class one elevation constant and blurring it: mountains were plateaus of equal height (heights spanned 89 to 142 where the base game reaches 239), the normal map was computed from that flat field, and rivers were random, disjoint, single-pixel paths that never joined (0.33% of land against the base game's 2.28%, ruler-straight on flat ground). A geography model (ADR 0055) names ranges with crests, passes and snow lines, volcanoes with craters, plateaus and chasms, rivers from a lake to a sea, and lakes; nothing could turn those into a heightmap or a river bitmap. Plan sections C6 and C7 of `docs/plans/zin-map-pipeline.md` design that synthesis; this ADR records what was built and the interface the later stages read.

## Decision

### Scope and opt-in

`create_world` with `geography` or `geographyPath` builds its land, heights, normal map, `rivers.bmp` and permanent-snow terrain from the model. A request without a geography is unchanged: the old elevation classes and drainage run exactly as before, and every existing test and fixture keeps its bytes. The new optional inputs are `relief` (`scale`: `vanilla`, `low` or `dramatic`; `detail`: 0 to 1) and `rivers` as `{ density }`; an integer `rivers` keeps its meaning without a geography and, with one, limits the number of unnamed river systems. Both are refused without a geography (`MAP_RELIEF_REQUIRES_GEOGRAPHY`, `MAP_RIVER_DENSITY_REQUIRES_GEOGRAPHY`).

Biomes, climate, fertility, region terrain shares and sea ice are the climate stage (C8) and are not synthesised. A model that declares any of them is blocked with `MAP_GEOGRAPHY_SYNTHESIS_UNAVAILABLE`, whose message and `details.unavailable` name the parts, instead of being generated with them silently ignored. The model preview reports `synthesis: 'available'` or `'unavailable'` and lists them in `unsupported`. With a reference picture the picture still supplies the coast and the terrain classes; the model supplies relief, lakes and rivers, and explicit geography wins over the picture's elevation and river hints.

### Pipeline (`src/hoi4_agent_tools/map/relief/`)

1. **Surface** (`land.ts`). Lands are rasterised from their polygons through a seeded fractal displacement bounded by `coastDetail` times 0.5% of the map width, lakes from theirs (a third of that), specks under 24 pixels sink or fill, and the edge policy (ADR 0061) and the lake rules (ADR 0053) run on the result. The synthesis starts from that final surface.
2. **Macro relief** (`macro-relief.ts`), on a grid of half the map size (3.3 million cells at 5,120 by 2,560). Base: a low-frequency fractal, a valley-scale texture and ridged creases around `100 + 38 * elevationBase`; plateaus blend to `96 + 148 * height` with a two-cell cliff or a ramp whose width follows the plateau's size (height 0 is the land floor, a chasm); ranges add `amplitude * profile(u) * envelope(t)` where `u` is the distance from the path over the half-width of that side. Profiles are `ridge` (sharp crest over a foothill apron), `massif` (flat shoulders), `escarpment` (a steep face on one side, a long back slope on the other) and `wall` (almost vertical sides); `asymmetry` widens one side; the footprint wanders in width and the crest keeps within 7% of its peak; the whole range is displaced sideways by a smooth noise so the crest meanders by up to 30% of its half-width; spurs leave the crest at 50 to 70 degrees; a pass lowers the crest by `depth` over a window of 2.2 pass widths; `snowline` marks permanent snow. Volcanoes are cones measured from their rim (`(1 - sqrt(s))^1.25`) with a bowl-shaped crater of `crater` radius and radial gullies. Crest height is `100 + 148 * peak * scale` (scale 0.75, 1 or 1.15), plateau height `96 + 148 * height * scale`; the result is capped at 252.
3. **Hydrology** (`hydrology.ts`), on the macro grid. Heights are conditioned by a priority flood whose rise per cell in depressions and flats is hashed, so flat ground drains along irregular trees, not straight lines to the nearest outlet. Named rivers are routed in parent-first order as least-cost paths (8-neighbour A\* or Dijkstra) from the source, or from the lowest or nearest free shore cell of a source lake, through the `via` points to the mouth (a point, a sea coast, a lake shore or a cell beside the parent); uphill steps cost heavily, the cells of other rivers and the cells beside them are off limits (goal cells and the first two cells from the source excepted, so the pixel paths, which bend a little, can stay apart), and a route is refused with `MAP_RIVER_UNREACHABLE_MOUTH` when it would climb more than 22 height units above the lowest point it has passed, or when no land route exists. Routes are then given smooth bends (displaced by noise, tapering at both ends, retried at half amplitude if they leave land or touch another river). The channels are burned into the heights and the flood is run again with the channels as outlets, so every other cell drains to the coast or into a channel; flow accumulation is weighted by an optional rainfall field (`rainfall`, the hook for C8). The accumulation network above a threshold chosen for the density budget (default 2% of land pixels, never more than 2.5% or 130,000 pixels) is cut into rivers: the main stem follows the larger catchment at each confluence, short stubs are pruned, long streams get the same bends, and `rivers: n` keeps the `n` largest systems. A `delta: true` river fans into two distributaries near its mouth. Widths follow catchment on a base-two logarithmic scale from 3 to 11, rise to a floor set by the declared `size`, and never narrow downstream (a river below a confluence is at least as wide as the tributary). Eight steps of stream-power carving (`0.06 * sqrt(catchment) * slope`), beds that never rise downstream, and valleys with flat floors and walls around the wider rivers shape the macro heights.
4. **Full-resolution heights** (`full-resolution.ts`). The carved macro field is upsampled bilinearly with the macro land mask, so the coast never sags to zero; ridged and fractal detail is added with an amplitude that grows with the mountain footprint (a few height units on plains, about six on the high ground at `detail` 0.5); the coast ramps from 96 at the shore to the inland height over six pixels. Heights stay floating point until the end, and strips of 64 rows give the cancellation points.
5. **River pixels** (`river-raster.ts`). Each macro river becomes an orthogonal one-pixel path by tracing its smoothed polyline, with small seeded bends added so the grid does not favour 45-degree and axis lines, and a short A\* repair where the trace meets water or another river. A river never comes within one pixel of another except at its join; a tributary ends in a red pixel (index 1) beside exactly one parent pixel, a distributary starts in a yellow pixel (index 2) beside its parent, the main branch of every connected river has exactly one green source (index 0), and loops and 2 x 2 blocks are cut. Beds are lowered so the river never runs uphill at full resolution, and unnamed headwaters beyond the pixel cap are dropped smallest first.
6. **Quantisation and normals** (`terrain-synthesis.ts`). Heights are rounded once, monotonically, after the rivers and the optional rim adjustment. Water is 89 with the lake bank of ADR 0053 (93 and 91 beside land); land is at least 96. The normal map is computed at half size from the float field with `r = 128 - 127 * nx / |n|` and a gain of 2.6 per height step, so the response where the encoding is linear matches the base game's -2.6; steep walls saturate, as any normal does.

Only `+ - * /`, `sqrt`, `floor`, `round` and integer arithmetic enter the result (no `exp`, `pow`, `sin` or `hypot`), so a seed gives the same terrain on every platform; a recorded-hash test enforces it.

### The interface for the partition (C9), the border metric (C10) and the climate stage (C8)

`synthesizeTerrain(input)` returns a `SynthesizedTerrain` (also `GeneratedWorld.terrain`), whose typed fields are documented in `relief/fields.ts`:

- `fields.barrier`: full size `strength` (0 to 255) and `sources` flags. Rivers by width 100 to 212 (`BARRIER_RIVER`), steep ground with a central gradient of 2 or more 60 to 200 (`BARRIER_STEEP`), the crest line 150 (`BARRIER_CREST`), lake shores 90 (`BARRIER_LAKE_SHORE`), impassable ranges 255 outside their pass gaps (`BARRIER_WALL`) and fort lines 180 (`BARRIER_FORT_LINE`). This is the barrier field `B` of C9 and the feature set of C10; the feature-aware partition reads its walls, fort lines and crests (`partitionFieldsWithTerrainLines`, ADR 0058) and takes rivers and steep ground from its own river-distance and slope planes.
- `fields.valley`: half size, how deeply rivers cut valleys and floodplains (0 to 255, 10 per height unit); fertility and settlement read it.
- `fields.crestDistance` and `fields.mountain`: half size distance to the nearest crest in pixels (clipped at 255) and a 0 to 255 mountain weight.
- `riverBitmap`, `riverOrder` and `riverPaths`: the `rivers.bmp` indices, the river order at each pixel and every river's pixel path from source to mouth, with `rivers` (id, name, pixels, sinuosity, mouth, parent, junction, width, order, completeness) for crossing counts and naming.
- `heights`, `normals`, `elevation` (the 0..1 land and -1..0 water scale the province generator expects) and `snow` (permanent snow above a range's snow line, which the terrain bitmap writes as index 16).
- `relief` and `riverStatistics`: percentiles, mean step, river pixels and share, counts and the straight share.

The hooks for C8 are `rainfall` (a half-size multiplier of one) and the ignored model parts above. `adjustHeight` lets the edge policy raise the rim in the macro relief before the hydrology, so rivers drain away from the rim instead of running uphill over it (this retires the `rim` ordering gap of ADR 0061 for geography worlds).

### Self-checks

Findings are generation-time diagnostics. `MAP_PASS_NOT_LOWER` (a gate that is not a gap) is an error; `MAP_RANGE_CREST_LOW` (a crest under 90% of its requested height outside the pass windows, searched within 40% of the half-width of the path because the crest meanders), `MAP_VOLCANO_NO_CRATER`, `MAP_RELIEF_FLAT` (a 99th percentile under 150 with ranges), `MAP_RIVER_STRAIGHT_RUN` (more than 3% of river pixels on straight stretches of 41 pixels; the base game measures 1.1% and this synthesis 1.0 to 1.6%), `MAP_RIVER_DENSITY` (over 3%, under 1% at the default, or the pixel cap hit), `MAP_RIVER_PIXEL_ROUTE_INCOMPLETE`, `MAP_LAKE_ENDORHEIC` and `MAP_RIVER_STREAMS_DROPPED` are warnings or info. `MAP_RIVER_UNREACHABLE_MOUTH` (error) refuses a named river that cannot reach its mouth. `MAP_RELIEF_SYNTHESIZED` reports the statistics and the first named rivers. These checks need the model, so `hoi4.map_inspect` cannot repeat them on an existing mod; the appearance validators of ADR 0054 still read every file written.

## Consequences

An agent can describe a world as ranges, volcanoes, plateaus, lakes and rivers and get a heightmap with crests, passes, cones and craters, a normal map with real shading, and drainage with confluences, tributaries, deltas, lake outlets and widths by catchment, in one call and under 10 seconds for the synthesis of a 5,120 by 2,560 map (6.5 seconds and about 450 MB on the review fixture). The fields give the partition, border and climate stages one typed source of truth.

The look is derived, not observed: height targets, the 2.6 gain and the river widths are calibrated to the base game's measured distributions, and the owner's in-game review after the colour and relief stages is the real test. Rivers in a very flat model still run in a few long loose bends; `relief.detail` and the model's own relief are the levers. Named rivers must be reachable over land: a brook that has to cross a wall is an error naming the climb, not a silent detour. Render layers for relief, slope and river order belong to the composite renders (C14).

Tests use a synthetic, project-owned model shaped like the ZIN draft (a wall with three gates, a ridge with spurs and a snow line, a closed ring, an escarpment, two volcanoes, a lake with an outflow, a river with two tributaries, a delta, a plateau and a chasm): determinism and recorded hashes; water and coast conventions; the height distribution; crests, passes, craters, plateaus; normal response and unit length; independent checks of the river bitmap (no 2 x 2 block, no isolated pixel, one source per component, every river ends in water, the edge or its parent, flows downhill and never narrows); fields; options (density cap, counts, scale, detail, rainfall, height adjustment); negative cases (a river over a wall, a river to another landmass, a model with errors); a flat-plateau regression for the straight-run metric; the world and planner integration including the edge band, the rim and a reference picture; and an MCP write.
