# ADR 0058: Feature-aware province, state and region partition

## Context

`create_world` cut provinces as a warped Voronoi diagram of jittered sites (`world-provinces.ts`) and grouped them into regions and states by breadth-first growth (`growClusters` in `world.ts`). Neither step could see a river, a ridge, a shore or a named region, and province size hardly varied. Measured with the border naturalness metric (ADR 0059), the borders of a generated mod map were at chance level against rivers and steep slopes (province, state and country enrichment 0.94, 0.70 and 0.50; vanilla 1.35, 1.40 and 1.46), and land provinces had a median of 780 pixels with a narrow spread (vanilla: median 160, p10 77, p90 846). The requirement is a partition whose borders prefer natural lines, whose sizes follow the land, and whose states and regions follow the same lines and the geography model. The relief and hydrology stages (ADR 0056) that will supply good ridge and valley fields are built in parallel, and the planned territory assignment service needs the province graph.

## Decision

The new partition is opt-in (`create_world.partition: 'feature-aware'`) and the default stays `legacy`, so every existing request keeps its bytes for a seed. A world given a geography model (ADR 0055) defaults to `feature-aware` (`resolvePartitionMode`). Since the relief and hydrology synthesis (ADR 0056) a valid model reaches that default; a model that declares the parts of the climate stage (biomes, climate, fertility, region terrain shares, sea ice) is still blocked with `MAP_GEOGRAPHY_SYNTHESIS_UNAVAILABLE` before any partition runs. The code is in `src/hoi4_agent_tools/map/partition*.ts` and `world-partition.ts`; the generator calls the same typed service any other code can call.

**Input contract.** `PartitionFields` (`partition-fields.ts`) holds one byte per pixel (two for the region) of distance to the nearest river, ridge strength, slope (gradient magnitude times 16), terrain class, geography region or named sea id, fertility and a model-drawn barrier, plus the land, sea and lake mask and a region table with the density each region asks for (`provinceAreaPx`, `provincesPerState`). `partitionFieldsFromBitmaps` computes a basic version from the `heightmap.bmp`, `terrain.bmp` and `rivers.bmp` a map already has (a chamfer distance with an east-west wrap, central-difference slope that treats water as level, a crest measure over four axes, a fertility estimate from terrain, river proximity and slope); `partitionFieldsFromGeography` adds regions, named seas and wall ranges from a resolved model; the generator builds fields from its own float rasters (`surfacePartitionFields`). For a world built from a geography model the generator calls `geographyPartitionFields` (`world-partition.ts`): slope and ridge are read from the synthesised `heightmap.bmp` values instead of the 0..1 elevation (which stops at height 206), `partitionFieldsFromGeography` adds the regions, named seas, their densities and the wall ranges, and `partitionFieldsWithTerrainLines` raises the barrier plane on the lines the synthesis drew from the model: impassable ranges outside their pass gaps (255, a hard line), fort lines (180) and range crests (150). Rivers and steep ground of the synthesis barrier are left to the river distance, slope and ridge planes, so they do not count twice. A wall range from the model is drawn along its whole path, pass gaps included, so no province straddles a gate either; the gate stays an adjacency between the provinces on its two sides. The synthesis valley field is not read yet: fertility still comes from terrain, river proximity and slope. Only `+ - * /` and `sqrt` are used, so the planes are identical on every platform.

**Provinces.** A barrier field B (0 to 255) takes the strongest of: rivers (255 on the river, 170 and 70 one and two pixels away), steep slopes (from 1 to 4 per pixel), ridge strength, wall ranges, lake shores, and on the sea a faint contour along the shelf edge. The cost of entering a pixel is `10 * (1 + beta * B / 255)` (14 for a diagonal move) with a smooth jitter so featureless borders are not straight bisectors, plus a penalty when the terrain class changes and a large one when the geography region changes. Seeds come from variable-radius dart throwing (ten to forty candidates per final seed, hashed priority, spacing `0.8 * sqrt(planned area)`) at a density that follows the planned area: land from the ground (fertile, hills, mountain, waste bands), blended towards the fertile band by fertility, reduced near the coast and rivers and scaled by the region's `provinceAreaPx`; sea from the distance to land (small on the shelf, large on the open sea); lakes at 6,000 pixels. When the bands are not given they are derived from `landProvincePixels` and `seaProvincePixels` and scaled so the mean planned area equals the request. A marker-controlled watershed (multi-source Dijkstra with integer costs in a circular bucket queue, ties to the first arrival) labels every pixel; wavefronts slow on natural lines and meet on them. Provinces that still straddle a river or wall are cut along it (pieces under 8 pixels rejoin), and a sea province that fills a channel no wider than `narrowStraitPx` between two land masses is cut along the middle by nearest land mass, so a narrow strait has two sea provinces across it. The labels then go through the same finishing passes as the legacy partition (`assembleWorldProvinces`: split disconnected and over-wide parts, absorb slivers, repair four-province corners, number by first pixel), which still enforce every engine rule: connected provinces, no box above an eighth of the map, no X crossing, at least 20 pixels where a same-kind neighbour exists, 65,000 provinces. The passes gain an optional group plane and line plane: merges and pixel moves prefer staying inside a region and not crossing a river or wall, and cross them only when no other repair exists.

**Graph, states and regions.** `buildProvinceGraph` builds, in one pass, a compressed-row graph with the shared border length and the mean barrier strength along it (the seam wraps) and `provinceEdgeCosts` turns it into directed Dijkstra costs; this is the service the planned territory assignment reuses. States and strategic regions are agglomerative clusters (`clusterProvinces`): the pair with the best affinity merges first, where affinity is the shared border damped by `1 / (1 + 12 * barrier)^2` (a hard line, barrier above 0.6, counts 5%), damped again between unlike ground, divided by the square root of the two perimeters, and tapered to zero as the merged size nears its maximum. Provinces of different geography regions never share a cluster, and a region's `provincesPerState` replaces the request's state size inside it. Sea regions follow named seas and prefer shelf with shelf.

**Opt-in options.** `create_world.partition` (`legacy` or `feature-aware`) and `create_world.partitionTuning` (`landArea.{fertile,hills,mountain,waste}`, `seaArea.{coastal,open}`, `provincesPerState`, `regionSize`, `barrierStrength`, `minProvincePixels`, `narrowStraitPx`). `partitionTuning` with `legacy` is refused. Contradictory bands (`MAP_PARTITION_AREA_BANDS_CONTRADICTORY`), reversed ranges, a map too small for the minimum province (`MAP_PARTITION_MAP_TOO_SMALL`) and a strength out of range are refused before any pixel is touched. A plan that needs more than 21,000 provinces fails with `MAP_PARTITION_PROVINCE_COUNT_HIGH` naming the fields to change (G-30, G-48); above 14,000 provinces both partitions add the warning of the same code to the world notes (for the legacy partition this is new: such a response gains the warning, its files are unchanged). A coast that touches itself only at corners (a 2 x 2 block whose diagonals are two kinds) cannot be cut into valid provinces, so before partitioning the generator turns one pixel of the weaker kind into the stronger (`MAP_PARTITION_MASK_SMOOTHED`, info). A geography world smooths before the terrain synthesis, so its heights, rivers and fields follow the smoothed mask (a pixel turned to land gets a land height); any other world smooths after its drainage and sets the changed pixels' elevation and river index, as before. The terrain synthesis and the feature-aware partition check the request's cancellation signal between rows and every 65,536 pixels of the watershed and stop with its reason.

## Consequences

Measured on project-owned synthetic fixtures (`tests/helpers/map-partition-fixture.ts`): on a 1,024 x 512 world with a dense river net, the border enrichment of province borders rises from 1.02 to 1.34 and of state borders from 0.98 to 2.22, and the share of state border length on a natural line from 0.24 to 0.55 (province borders are limited by the length of the features: a river can be the border of at most about the river length). The regression test fails if the new partition does not beat the old one on such a fixture. On a 5,120 x 2,560 fixture with 400 and 2,500 pixel provinces the legacy partition makes 25,514 provinces (enrichment 0.97 and 0.90); the feature-aware one makes 17,061 (land mean 399 pixels, p10 228, median 320, p90 507, maximum 4,535; sea mean 2,345, p10 678, p90 4,161; enrichment 1.75 and 3.38; natural share of state borders 0.03 to 0.12 on a map whose natural lines cover 3.4% of the land). It takes about 9 seconds and a peak of 0.83 GiB resident, and the complete `generateWorld` takes 20 seconds at 0.79 GiB (legacy: 19 seconds). The base rate is low because the fixture has few steep slopes at that scale.

The old partition stays selectable and unchanged. A province can still straddle a river or wall after the four-corner repair has had to join two provinces (a few percent of crossings in the tests); the repairs prefer not to. Sea provinces can exceed their planned size because the Dijkstra, not the plan, decides the final cell. Province count is only checked against the plan before partitioning and against 14,000 after, so a map near the limit can still be refused late by the text table limit. The wiki's limit of about 21,000 provinces is a wiki value (W), not verified here. Tuning of `barrierStrength` and the band defaults is aesthetic and meant for owner review after the relief and river stages exist. The legacy partition, the finishing passes and the later world stages (states, countries, files) do not check the cancellation signal; a cancelled request stops at the next check after them. The mask smoothing alters at most a few coast pixels of the generated land and is only done when the generator builds the fields; callers passing their own fields must give a mask without such corners (`smoothDiagonalContacts` is exported). Provinces are partitioned before terrain is chosen per province, so province terrain still comes from the old rule (elevation quantiles, moisture, latitude, or the reference majority); the fertility and ground fields only shape sizes and clustering.
