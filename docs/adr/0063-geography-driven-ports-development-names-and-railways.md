# ADR 0063: Geography-driven ports, development, names and railways

## Context

`create_world` put ports in a random 35% of states, rolled population, buildings and resources per state, named states "District N" and strategic regions "Region N" unless a label landed inside, and joined supply nodes by a railway that ignored slope, rivers and ranges. The ZIN program records Aenor as weak and Zol as rich, the reverse of its lore, and 941 of 1,332 states carried a placeholder. Plan section C13 asks for the same facts to come from the geography model.

## Decision

For a world built from a geography model the economy stage (`src/hoi4_agent_tools/map/gameplay/economy.ts`) runs after states and owners exist; `create_world.development: { model: 'geography' | 'legacy' }` (default `geography` when a geography is given; `legacy` keeps the random economy and still gets the crossings of ADR 0062). It uses no random numbers, so a run is a function of the model and the seed. A world without a geography is untouched, byte for byte.

**Harbours and ports** (`ports.ts`). Each coastal land province gets a score in 0 to 1: 0.28 concavity (water share in a disc of 0.6% of the width around the coast point, lower is a bay), 0.20 shelter (the same in a disc of 2%), 0.14 river mouth width, 0.10 depth class of the sea it faces (shelf 1, open 0.6, abyss 0.3; an inland sea uses its lake's class), 0.18 nearby sites (a port site in the province 1, a city 0.5, neighbouring province 0.6 of that), 0.10 coast length. A state gets a port at its best province when the state holds a port site (the site's province, or the nearest harbour within 3% of the width), when it holds the country capital, or when its best score is in the top 45% of coastal states; every coastal territory of a country keeps at least one port. The level is `1 + round(3 * score) + max(0, importance - 2) + 1 for the capital`, at most 6; a level 3 or a capital port adds a dockyard where a slot is free. A port site on a decorative lake or inland raises `MAP_PORT_LAKE_UNSUPPORTED` or `MAP_PORT_SITE_NOT_COASTAL` and places nothing. The ports of an inland sea (ADR 0062) are ordinary ports on a sea province, which closes plan open question 1 for the model (the engine's treatment of a naval base next to an inland sea is unverified, U).

**Development** (`development.ts`, `metrics.ts`). Fertility per province is the model's statement (a land's `fertility`, then biomes from low to high `z`, each with its `fertility` or its kind's default) when it covers half the province, else the province terrain's default, plus 0.2 times the valley the rivers cut and minus a slope term above height 140. A province scores 0.55 fertility, 0.12 river access, 0.10 coast, 0.06 lake shore, 0.08 gate, 0.3 site strength and minus 0.15 mountain share. A state's development is the area-weighted mean plus 0.25 of its strongest site and 0.25 for a country capital; manpower is `pixels * 180 * max(0.08, development)`, the category follows `development * 5.2` plus site and capital terms (wasteland only where fertility is under 0.1 and nothing stands), infrastructure and factories follow the development and the category's slots, a capital has an air base. Victory points come from the sites of kind capital, city, port and fortress (importance 1 to 5 gives 3, 5, 8, 12 or 20; the capital province 25); a state without one gets a small point on its best province. Provinces with 8 or more points are urban. Resources are not terrain rolls: a range gives its profile's ore (ridge and wall steel, massif tungsten, escarpment chromium) to the third of the provinces near its path with most mountain, in the amount `2 + 6 * peak`; a `mine` site gives its `resource` (new optional site field: steel, aluminium, tungsten, chromium, oil, rubber; steel if unnamed) in the amount `2 + 3 * importance`; the richest 40% of jungle provinces give rubber. A site with a `country` anchor chooses that country's capital province.

**Names** (`names.ts`). A state is named by its strongest settlement site, else a lake shore ("X Shore"), a river valley, a range's foothills, a bay or cape coast, a geography region, a land; a strategic region by the region, land, sea or inland lake holding most of its area; a victory point by its site, else the river it stands on ("X Ford"), the lake shore ("X Landing"), a place or "<state> Town". Articles are dropped when composing. Names shared by several features get a compass adjective by position within the group (Northern, Southeastern, Central), then numerals; a lake province gets `PROV<id>` with the lake's name. Reference labels given by the caller win. A feature the model gives no name falls back to "<Country> District N", "Region N" or "<Country> Settlement N" and is counted: `MAP_NAME_PLACEHOLDER` (warning, with the counts by kind and the share) whenever there is one. Rivers are not named in game; their names enter state and victory-point names.

**Railways** (`rail-cost.ts`, `derived.ts`). The cost of a step is the distance between province centres in units of the mean land province, times the ground (urban 0.8, plains 1, forest 1.25, desert 1.3, hills 1.6, jungle 2, marsh 2.2, mountain 3), plus the height difference and the climb of the border over the higher province (per 12 height units), plus 1.1 for a river on the border, times 0.7 at a gate and a discount along a model road. `planSupplyRebuild` takes it as an optional `railCost` (with `trunk`, the cities of 8 or more points, whose lines get level 2); impassable rows are never crossed, so a wall is crossed only at a gate. Without `railCost` the planner is unchanged.

## Consequences

The economy follows the model's inputs rather than the game design: balance (manpower scale, slots, resource amounts) is a plausible default the owner tunes, not a decision of this tool. Without biome or climate synthesis (C8) the terrain default stands in for fertility wherever the model states none, and `unsupportedGeographyParts` still blocks a model that declares biomes, climate or fertility at the operation; the economy reads those fields where a caller supplies them. Port levels, the 45% rule and the weights are calibrated on synthetic fixtures. Names depend on how much the model names; a model that names only lands gives compass variants of land names.

Regressions: harbour scores ranking bays above points, ports on port sites and inland-sea shores, a port on a decorative lake or inland refused with its code, development ranking fertile states above barren ones (Spearman over 0.6), ore from the mine site and the range, determinism, the legacy model unchanged, every state, region and victory point named and unique, placeholders counted exactly, railway costs symmetric and higher over mountains, railways never over a wall, and the full planner producing positions, supply and railways that validate.
